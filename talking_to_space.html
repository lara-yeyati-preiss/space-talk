Talking to space

Since the beginning of the space age, people have sent beyond Earth not only instruments and machines, but also something more intimate: an archive of humanity—
of who we are and who we were.

In 1972, NASA attached two gold-anodized aluminum plaques to a pair of spacecraft leaving the Solar System, in case they were intercepted by intelligent extraterrestrial life. Engraved on them are a man and a woman, a pulsar map locating our Sun, and a diagram of the hydrogen atom.

Pioneer Plaques

Two years later, astronomers transmitted a 1,679-bit binary message toward a star cluster in Hercules, 25,000 light-years away. Encoded within it: the numbers one through ten, the atomic numbers of the elements of life, the double helix, a diagram of our Solar System, a human figure.

Arecibo Message

In 1977, two spacecraft left Earth carrying gold-plated copper records into space, containing 116 images, greetings in 55 languages, and sounds, including a mother’s kiss, footsteps, and a heartbeat.

Voyager Golden Record
